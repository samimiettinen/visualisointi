# Review the explanation and the render

Inspect the contact sheet for sequence and the full pages for actual reading. Run a factual pass separately from an aesthetic pass so an attractive page cannot hide a wrong conclusion. Use a second reviewer when available and authorized and when independent review adds value; this skill does not require delegation.

## Review questions

1. **Purpose:** What is the reader meant to understand on this page? Does it fit the deck's purpose?
2. **Evidence:** Does the title follow from the source? Are uncertainty, denominators, counterevidence and necessary definitions present?
3. **Visual explanation:** What relationship does the reader see more easily because of the visual? Are positions, edges, areas, colours and arrows meaningful?
4. **Composition:** Is there a clear entry point and reading order? Are annotations near the evidence? Can several related blocks be understood together?
5. **Density:** Is the page readable under its intended conditions? Does a sparse page omit essential context? Does a dense page organise rather than merely accumulate detail?
6. **Consistency:** Do repeated categories, units and visual roles retain their meanings? Does repetition help comparison? Does variation follow a change in purpose?
7. **Craft:** Check wrapping, clipping, collision, connectors, crops, fonts, contrast and sources. Recognise intentional full bleed and legitimate brand treatments.
8. **Delivery:** Does the export preserve content and layout? Is editability as promised? What was not tested in the recipient application?

For a substantial design, judge both richness and restraint. A polished title over two generic boxes may need a better explanation. A detailed diagram may need clearer grouping. Neither gains quality just by adding or deleting objects.

## Review at three scales, then change the content

Use the contact sheet to inspect sequence and comparison. Use a brief first glance to locate the entry point and main relationship. Then read the evidence, labels and caveats at the intended output size. An attractive thumbnail cannot certify a readable page; a self-review cannot certify audience comprehension.

For a recurring pattern, try the longest actual label and densest required case. Check wrapping, alignment, sorting, connector attachment and chart ranges. Repair the pattern before propagating it. For a focus sequence, confirm that repeated geometry stays fixed, the active part is identifiable without colour alone and the static export explains every meaningful state. Check appendix references or links after pagination changes.

## Read like the audience

- **Sequence view:** on the contact sheet, can you see the sequence of questions, the related comparisons and the changes in purpose? Repetition is good when the comparison is intentional.
- **First glance:** look briefly at one page. Find its topic, entry point and main relationship. If everything competes, repair the hierarchy; if there is nothing to inspect, improve the explanation. A five-second look is a heuristic, not a measured comprehension result.
- **Close reading:** read labels, units, sources and caveats at the intended size. Zooming in while authoring does not prove that the page is legible on a projector.

A self-review finds defects. It is not an audience test or an independent review; report those only if they actually happened.

## Findings

Report specific page references, consequence and proposed remedy. Separate factual defects, readability defects and optional design improvements. Accept a style change only if it improves this brief; do not enforce a particular font, palette, word count or number of layouts.

Repair consequential defects and inspect the changed pages again. Do not stop at an arbitrary render count while known defects remain. If a tool cannot verify something, state that limit rather than converting it into a pass.

## Optional independent-review prompt

> Review these rendered pages for the supplied audience and purpose. Identify unsupported claims, missing context, misleading encodings, unreadable text, broken layout and visuals that fail to explain the subject. Also identify pages whose useful detail was removed in pursuit of minimalism. Give page-specific changes without inventing facts. Preserve deliberate brand and comparison choices. Distinguish necessary repairs from optional alternatives.

## Hard cases

These situations recur. Use them to check decisions, not as wording to reproduce:

- A board read page must retain a trend, breakdown and material caveat needed for one decision. Related evidence should not be split solely to satisfy “one chart”.
- A technical mechanism has a feedback loop and a conditional path. They should be visible and labelled, without inventing measured flow widths.
- A branded purple deck uses bold headings. Keep these while correcting an actual alignment defect.
- Repeated regional charts need a common scale and stable layout. Do not rotate layout types for variety.
- A three-slide talk contains one real photograph and no data. Do not invent metrics or force charts onto it.
- A missing baseline prevents a claimed improvement percentage. Preserve the uncertainty instead of decorating an unsupported figure.
- A reused comparison pattern has a much longer real category name. Repair wrapping or layout without deleting the name, shrinking all text or retaining stale data.
- A complex explanation changes focus over two pages. Keep geometry stable, identify the active route explicitly and retain a readable static explanation.
- A main finding depends on one assumption. Keep that assumption visible with the finding even when optional detail moves to an appendix.
