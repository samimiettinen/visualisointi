# Human design references

Reviewed 1 November 2026. These are human-authored design guides and editorial examples, not AI slide galleries. The descriptions below are a synthesis for this skill; the linked authors do not endorse this package. Inspect a relevant example when it would change a design choice, rather than loading the entire list on every task.

## The most useful guides

| Reference | What to inspect | Application to this skill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Nancy Duarte: Slidedocs](https://www.duarte.com/resources/books/slidedocs/) | Visual documents designed to be read without a presenter; links to templates and dense-slide examples | Treat self-read pages as information design. Preserve enough context for independent reading. |
| [Financial Times: Visual Vocabulary](https://github.com/Financial-Times/chart-doctor/blob/main/visual-vocabulary/README.md) | The chart families and the question each serves | Choose the relationship before choosing a chart. Broaden the repertoire beyond bars, lines and big figures. |
| [ONS: small multiple charts](https://service-manual.ons.gov.uk/data-visualisation/chart-types/small-multiple-charts) | The overlapping-line example and the panel alternative | Repetition can improve comparison. Use common scales, logical ordering and a reference series where helpful. |
| [Michael Alley / Penn State: assertion–evidence](https://www.assertion-evidence.com/) | Technical-talk tutorials, templates and model talks | Pair a supported message with visual evidence. Apply the method to technical assertions without forcing every reference or explanatory page into a claim. |
| [Storytelling with Data: chart guide](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/chart-guide) | Alternatives such as dot plots, bullet graphs and waterfalls alongside standard charts | Select a form people can read accurately; use annotation and comparison to make the evidence useful. |
| [The Data Visualisation Catalogue](https://datavizcatalogue.com/) | Search by function, including flow, hierarchy, networks and illustration diagrams | Add forms for relationships and mechanisms, not just numerical business charts. |
| [Ilia Blinderman / The Pudding: design](https://pudding.cool/process/how-to-make-dope-shit-part-2/) | Learning from existing work and iterating a visual explanation | Keep references for specific design problems. Transfer techniques into original work rather than copying surface styling. |

### Concrete examples to open

- **[The Pudding: Pockets, by Jan Diehm and Amber Thomas, August 2018](https://pudding.cool/2018/08/pockets/).** The subject becomes the visual: pocket outlines, comparable dimensions and object-fit exploration. The methods distinguish measured dimensions from generated curves. Transfer the idea of making the actual object carry the comparison. A slide version might use one annotated pair or a small set of matched silhouettes. Do not copy its artwork or reuse its historical findings as current facts. The cover and pocket-outline comparison were visually inspected for this revision.
- **[ONS: small multiple charts](https://service-manual.ons.gov.uk/data-visualisation/chart-types/small-multiple-charts).** The page supplies examples for both country-first and category-first panels. The choice changes which comparison is easiest. A deck can retain several repeated analytical pages when this supports the audience's task.
- **[Duarte: improving dense slides](https://www.duarte.com/resources/guides-tools/how-to-improve-dense-slides-with-slidedocs/).** A route to real before/after cases for read material. The public overview was inspected; the downloadable guide was not downloaded or reviewed. Do not claim its detailed layouts were validated here.
- **[Assertion–evidence model talks](https://www.assertion-evidence.com/models.html).** Useful for studying how technical drawings, diagrams and other evidence support a spoken explanation. The collection page was checked; the full talks were not watched in this revision.

## How the revision uses these references

The package's visual-selection table, review questions and example atlas are original synthesis. The aim is to expand what a page can explain while retaining readable hierarchy and trustworthy evidence. The sources support different choices; they do not form a universal aesthetic or a claim that more detail is always better.

Third-party text, illustrations, fonts and templates remain subject to their own terms. This package links to them and does not bundle their artwork. Package terms are in [LICENSE](../LICENSE).

For the practitioner discussions, adoption decisions and a working design routine, read [community-practice.md](community-practice.md).
