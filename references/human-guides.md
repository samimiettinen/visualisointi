# External examples to open

Open one of these when a concrete example would change a design decision, not on every task. Inspect the specific visual, state the transferable technique in one sentence, then build an original page from the user's evidence. Never copy artwork, layouts or findings from these pages.

| Resource | Open it for | What to take from it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Duarte: Slidedocs](https://www.duarte.com/resources/books/slidedocs/) | Pages designed to be read without a presenter | Self-read pages are information design; give them enough context to stand alone. |
| [Duarte: improving dense slides](https://www.duarte.com/resources/guides-tools/how-to-improve-dense-slides-with-slidedocs/) | Before-and-after treatment of dense read material | Organise density instead of cutting it. |
| [Financial Times: Visual Vocabulary](https://github.com/Financial-Times/chart-doctor/blob/main/visual-vocabulary/README.md) | Chart families grouped by the question they answer | Choose the relationship first, then the chart. |
| [ONS: small multiple charts](https://service-manual.ons.gov.uk/data-visualisation/chart-types/small-multiple-charts) | Overlapping lines versus a panel grid; country-first and category-first panels | Panel order decides which comparison is easiest; share scales. |
| [Assertion–evidence](https://www.assertion-evidence.com/) and its [model talks](https://www.assertion-evidence.com/models.html) | Technical talks where a supported claim sits over visual evidence | Pair the title's claim with evidence on the page; descriptive titles stay valid for explanation pages. |
| [Storytelling with Data: chart guide](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/chart-guide) | Dot plots, bullet graphs and waterfalls beside standard charts | Pick the form people read accurately; annotate where it matters. |
| [The Data Visualisation Catalogue](https://datavizcatalogue.com/) | Flow, hierarchy, network and illustration diagrams searchable by function | Forms for relationships and mechanisms, not only business charts. |
| [Datawrapper: text in data visualisations](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/text-in-data-visualizations) | Direct labels, units and annotations | Put words next to what they describe. |
| [The Pudding: Pockets](https://pudding.cool/2018/08/pockets/) | The object itself carrying the comparison (matched outlines) | A slide can compare real silhouettes or one annotated pair; the findings are historical, not current facts. |

Third-party text, illustrations, fonts and templates remain under their own terms. The skill links to them and bundles none of their material.
